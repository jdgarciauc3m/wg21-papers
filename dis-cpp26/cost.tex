\section{Cost of contracts removal}

\subsection{Removing the contracts feature}

In contrast with what it is stated in P4433R0~\cite{p4433r0}, the removal is mostly 
editorial.
We believe that such removal consists of the following actions:

\begin{itemize}

\item Removal of section 6.4.10 (\texttt[basic.scope.contract]).

\item Removal of section 6.11 (\texttt[basic.contract]).

\item Removal of section 8.9 (\texttt[stmt.contract.assert]).

\item Removal of section 9.4 (\texttt[dcl.contract]).

\item Removal of section 16.4.6.15 (\texttt[res.contract.assertions]).

\item Removal of section 17.10 (\texttt[support.contract]).

\item Removal of cross references to those sections.

\end{itemize}

\vspace{1em}
\begin{tcolorbox}[
  colback=blue!8!white,
  colframe=blue,
  title={\textbf{Cost of removing contracts}},
  breakable,
  width=\textwidth,
  boxrule=0.5pt
  ]
In summary, the removal is small, concentrated, and cleanly separable. According
to Doumler et al.~\cite{p4433r0}, contracts wording total for 4,946 words. The
whole standard contains around one million words. That is less than $0.5\%$ of
the total text of the standard.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Removing the dependency in hardened implementation}

Besides that, the specification of the hardened implementation needs to be
decoupled from contracts. That is not so complicated as it might seem, as the
initial specification of the hardened implementation did not include such
coupling.

In fact, the change requires modification of:

\begin{itemize}

\item
Modification of one sentence in 4.1.1 (\texttt[intro.compliance.general]).

\item
Modification of one sentence in 16.3.2.4 (\texttt[structure.specifications]).

\end{itemize}

\vspace{1em}
\begin{tcolorbox}[
  colback=blue!8!white,
  colframe=blue,
  title={\textbf{Cost of removing hardening dependency}},
  breakable,
  width=\textwidth,
  boxrule=0.5pt
  ]
The cost of this change is minimal as it requires changing two sentences in the
standard.
\end{tcolorbox}
