c) when the approval criteria of 2.6.3 are not met:
\begin{enumerate}
\item to circulate the revised enquiry draft for eight weeks voting (see 2.6.1), or
\item to circulate the revised committee draft for comments, or
\item to circulate a revised draft as a draft Technical Specification, or
\item to cancel the project, subject to a decision by the committee.
\end{enumerate}

In case 3), the project shall start at the approval stage

Following, we briefly analyze each option:

\subsection{Revised draft for enquiry}

The ISO/IEC directives specifically state \emph{``to circulate the revised
enquiry draft for eight weeks voting''} which means to revise the text of the
draft international standard after \emph{``at a meeting, address any comments
received and prepare, as needed, a revised version''}.

The WG21 might very well (as suggested by~\cite{p4433r0}), as a group, decide
not to make modifications regarding on the single matter where 11 National
Bodies have expressed a formal request to remove a feature from the standard.
However, not doing anything at all and expecting that positions of National
Bodies will change does not seem a very likely scenario.

In our opinion, if WG21 decides not to make any change regarding contracts the
result might be similar to the already expressed.

There is another possible course of action,though. If the WG21 removes the
contracts feature from the draft international standard, that would certainly
remove most, if not all, opposition and a new ballot would approve the standard.
It is very relevant to remark that taking this course would increase consensus.

\textbf{Note}: The document \emph{SoV and Collated Comments}~\cite{sov:comments}
explicitly states in its text \emph{``The DIS ballot was not approved. The
results and comments have been sent to the Project Editor and WG 21 to review,
prepared the final DoC, and provide an updated draft text for further
processing.''}

\subsection{Revised draft for comments}

The second option is \emph{``to circulate the revised committee draft for
comments''}. This is a variation of the previous option where after discussing
in a meeting the revised draft it is sent for a committee draft commenting
instead. That would require that National Bodies send a new batch of comments
and later a new DIS ballot is initiated.

This variation of previous option only introduces an extra delay over it, while
we do not see any clear advantage which would increase consensus among National
Bodies.

\subsection{Revised draft as a TS}

The third option is to abandon the idea of having a new standard and making the
new version a Technical Specification. 

This option might seem like a palatable compromise, but it doesn't actually do
anything about the problem of unresolved concerns still remaining unresolved.
They would now merely be unresolved in a TS instead of an IS, but that doesn't
solve the problem

Moreover, by taking this option we would be postponing many highly waited
features with little if any disagreement.

\subsection{Project cancellation}

We strongly believe that nobody wants this option to happen. However, if no
other option can be taken we could end up here. In this case, we would be back
to the start position, our baseline would be C++23 and we would have to start
from there in a new project.
